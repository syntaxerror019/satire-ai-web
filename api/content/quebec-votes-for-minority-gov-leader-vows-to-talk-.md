During a victory speech that somehow lasted 33 seconds, the Parti Québécois leader paused mid-monologue to address English-speaking Quebecers, who were presumably confused but also slightly impressed.  

“Quebecers face the same challenges,” Plamondon proclaimed, while simultaneously texting Ottawa with a series of cryptic emojis that likely meant “I’m sorry, but I’m not sorry.” The speech, which included a moment of silence for the French language and a spontaneous interpretive dance, was met with applause from anglophones who were either thrilled or just really tired.  

CBC News, ever the responsible outlet, has projected that this minority government will function with the efficiency of a group of cats arguing over who gets to sit on the windowsill. Plamondon, meanwhile, has promised to solve problems “together,” which in his world probably means sending everyone a group chat and hoping for the best.  

As the dust settles on this historic election, one thing is clear: Quebec has chosen a leader who will govern with the transparency of a magician’s rabbit and the decisiveness of a spreadsheet. Welcome to the future, where politics is just a series of misunderstandings and very polite disagreements.
