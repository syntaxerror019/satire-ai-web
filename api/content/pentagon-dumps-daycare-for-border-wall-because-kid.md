In a stunning display of fiscal responsibility, the Pentagon has decided that the best way to secure America’s southern border is to take money from military schools, daycares, and fire stations and use it to build a wall so tall, so wide, and so aggressively marketed that it will probably be the most expensive monument to human hubris since the Great Wall of China was briefly considered a viable career path by a group of confused Roman engineers.  

The move, hailed as a “masterstroke of logistics” by a Pentagon official who was paid in monopoly money, comes despite the fact that the military already has a wall—called “the budget”—that’s been holding things together for decades. But why stop there? Why not take the money meant for children’s lunches and use it to build a wall that will presumably be funded by children’s lunches? It’s a win-win!  

Among the projects being axed are a new elementary school in Germany, a daycares for Air Force One staff, and a fire station for Marines in South Carolina. The Pentagon insists this isn’t a last-minute decision; it’s just a “strategic reallocation of resources” that’s been in the works since the first time someone said the word “border” in a meeting that also included the words “funding” and “not this again.”  

The wall, which will stretch 140 miles and somehow not be called a wall, will include “enhancements” like replacing existing fences with “newer versions” and “adding layers” to existing walls. Because nothing says “security” like a wall that’s already there, but with more walls.  

Meanwhile, landowners along the Rio Grande are thrilled. Not because they’re getting a wall built behind their homes, but because the engineers are finally admitting that building a wall along a river is a terrible idea. “We’re just going to put it 50 yards back,” one engineer explained, “so the river can still flow, but also feel like it’s being watched by a giant, 30-foot-tall, 200-mile-long version of your ex’s Instagram profile.”  

As for the people who actually live near the wall? Well, they’re being offered “security gates” and “passcodes” to keep their land from turning into a desert of legal paperwork and existential dread. Because nothing says “safe and secure” like being trapped on a ranch with no escape route and a 50% chance that the local drug cartel will use your passcode to smuggle in a truckload of existential dread.  

In the end, the Pentagon says it’s all for the children. The children who will grow up learning to climb walls instead of reading, who will have their nap times replaced by drills on how to build walls, and who will one day look back on this moment and think, “Wow, I guess the government really did prioritize walls over everything else.” And maybe that’s okay. After all, what’s a child’s education without a little wall-building?
