In a stunning act of bureaucratic negligence, 2021’s country, Americana, folk, and bluegrass festivals have been rescheduled to occur simultaneously with the next global pandemic. Organizers, in a move that can only be described as “bold,” are now offering “safe” attendance options that include mandatory vaccination checks, social distancing zones shaped like pyramids, and a new genre of music called “quarantine-core,” which consists of nothing but a single sustained cough.  

This year’s lineup features a who’s who of musical legends, including Johnny Cash (who will finally be buried in a biohazard bag after his 2020 performance at the “Coughing at the Crossroads” festival), Dolly Parton (now performing exclusively via telepathy to avoid spreading germs), and a surprise set by Taylor Swift, who will be streaming her performance from a sealed submarine.  

Festivals that were originally scheduled for the winter and spring have been “pushed back” to the summer, which is technically still “this year” if you ignore the fact that we’ve already used up all our collective hope for 2021. Some events have been canceled entirely, though organizers insist this is just a “strategic pivot” rather than an admission of failure. These festivals will return in 2022, which is apparently a time when people will suddenly remember how to enjoy live music without accidentally breathing on each other.  

For those eager to attend, tickets are now available via a new platform called “VaxPass,” which requires users to upload a photo of their vaccination card, a screenshot of their Spotify playlist, and a video of them dancing alone in a room while shouting “I’M NOT AFRAID OF CORONAVIRUS!” at the top of their lungs.  

If you’re wondering why this year’s festivals are all happening in abandoned malls and parking garages, it’s because the organizers couldn’t afford to rent actual venues. But don’t worry—they’ve secured a $10 million grant from the CDC, which they’re using to build a “sanitized” stage made entirely of disinfectant wipes.
