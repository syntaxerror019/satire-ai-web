Ladies, gentlemen, and anyone who’s ever accidentally clicked on a benchmark test while trying to find a cat video, prepare to have your lives upended by the long-awaited arrival of *Bench 2.0* — the latest innovation from Tom’s Hardware, now exclusively available to Premium subscribers who’ve presumably never heard of the word “free.”  

Yes, the original *Bench* tool was “merely okay,” a sad relic of the past that forced users to sort through “hundreds of products every year, thousands of tests, tens of thousands of data points” like a 1990s librarian who’s never heard of a search bar. But now, thanks to *Bench 2.0*, you can finally organize your chaos into something resembling a spreadsheet!  

The new and improved *Bench 2.0* features a “detailed hierarchy” for tests, allowing you to filter benchmarks by categories like “gaming,” “synthetic,” or “whatever that one app was that crashed your computer.” It also includes “detailed product pages” — because nothing says “trustworthy” like a website that tells you a GPU costs $1,500 but doesn’t mention it’s made of “mystery components sourced from a black market in the 1980s.”  

But the real showstopper? The “Bang for the Buck” charts, which plot performance against cost — a revolutionary feature that’s basically the most important thing since sliced bread. Now you can finally determine whether a $1,200 GPU is worth it by comparing it to a $200 GPU that somehow outperforms it in every test. Because nothing says “value” like a chart that makes you question your life choices.  

And for those of you who’ve already bought an RTX 4070 Ti (or are just *pretending* you have one), *Bench 2.0* lets you “flag it as your own” and track it in your “My Rig” section, which is just a fancy way of saying you can now stalk your own hardware like it’s a social media influencer.  

Oh, and did we mention you can now share your benchmark results? Because nothing says “I’m a tech enthusiast” like embedding a fully interactive widget into your LinkedIn profile to show off how your $3,000 PC outperforms a $300 PC in “17 different ways that are all somehow confusing.”  

Finally, *Bench 2.0* even has “light and dark modes” — because why should your eyes have to suffer when you’re already paying $29 a month for a tool that lets you compare the performance of a 10-year-old laptop to a brand-new one?  

So, in conclusion: *Bench 2.0* is now live, and it’s the only way to experience the thrill of benchmarking without ever having to leave the comfort of your Premium subscription. Don’t miss out — for just $29, you’ll get access to the most useless, over-engineered, and unnecessarily complicated tool ever created.  

(And if you’re not a Premium subscriber? Well, you’re just going to have to live with the horror of not knowing whether your GPU is worth it. Good luck.)
