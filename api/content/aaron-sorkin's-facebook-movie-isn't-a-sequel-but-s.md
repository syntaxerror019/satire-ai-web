The new film starring Jeremy Strong as Mark Zuckerberg and Mikey Madison as a whistleblower who accidentally invented the internet is here to remind us that nothing about the 2010s was actually real. Directed by the man who once made a movie about Facebook that somehow managed to ignore the fact that it’s a company that sells your data to creepy ads, *The Social Reckoning* is less a film and more a 137-minute PowerPoint slide titled “How Not To Ruin The Internet.”  

Critics are already calling it “the cinematic equivalent of a Meta press release written by a caffeine-deprived intern,” while one reviewer claimed the film’s most impressive feat is making Jeremy Allen White’s performance as a journalist feel like a side quest in a video game. The plot, which involves a series of loosely connected scenes where characters yell about “ethics” and “user engagement,” is said to be “so vague it could be a metaphor for modern life—or just a really bad screenplay.”  

The movie’s audio version, generated by AI, is reportedly so poorly executed that it sounds like a robotic version of a confused college student who accidentally pressed record during a Zoom call. As one listener put it, “It’s like listening to a podcast where the host is a sentient Alexa trying to explain cryptocurrency to a goldfish.”  

Meanwhile, the *Commotion* podcast panel debated whether the film is a “self-aware satire” or “just a really expensive way to tell people they’re wrong about everything.” Vass Bednar, the public policy expert, reportedly said, “This movie is like if Mark Zuckerberg and Frances Haugen had a baby, and the baby grew up to be a corporate lawyer who still thinks TikTok is a real thing.”  

If you’ve ever scrolled through Facebook and wondered why your ex is still friends with your mom, this is the movie for you.
