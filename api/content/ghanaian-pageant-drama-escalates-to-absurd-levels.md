In a stunning twist that will leave you questioning everything you know about beauty pageants and the human condition, Jocelyn Bioh has returned to the stage to play Eloise Amponsah—a pageant scout so obsessed with finding the “perfect” Miss Ghana contestant that she’s reportedly considered hiring a team of AI algorithms to analyze the candidates’ pores for “soul depth.” The Broadway production, *School Girls; Or, the African Mean Girls Play*, is less about teenage girl drama and more about a surreal, high-stakes competition where contestants are judged on their ability to recite Shakespearean sonnets while balancing a tray of jollof rice on their heads.  

Bioh, who previously took a nine-year hiatus from acting to “focus on writing a play about how deeply insecure I am,” claims she felt “zero nerves” during her comeback performance. This is despite the fact that her character, Eloise, is so consumed by her own insecurities that she once tried to bribe a contestant with a lifetime supply of hair relaxers. “I want for once in my life to finally be seen,” Eloise declares, a line that has been interpreted by critics as either a profound statement on self-acceptance or a desperate cry for attention from a woman who once tried to compete in a beauty pageant herself and was disqualified for “over-ironing her dress.”  

The play’s cast, which includes Tony nominee Jasmine Amy Rogers (who previously played a sentient toaster in *The Great British Bake Off: The Musical*) and *The Gilded Age* star Denée Benton (who once starred in a Broadway production about a sentient teapot), has been praised for its “unrelenting commitment to absurdity.” Patina Miller, who plays a contestant with “a personality so large it could power a small country,” has been spotted offstage wearing a hat made entirely of glitter and a T-shirt that reads “I’m Not Racist, I Just Hate Your Hair.”  

As for the play’s message? It’s unclear. But one thing is certain: If this is what happens when Black women are given a platform to explore their deepest fears, insecurities, and obsession with pageantry, then the world is in for a very long, very glittery ride.
