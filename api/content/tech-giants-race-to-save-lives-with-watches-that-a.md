In a stunning turn of events, the world’s leading tech companies have united to revolutionize healthcare by creating smartwatches that somehow, against all odds, manage to measure blood pressure. Who knew a device designed to track steps and remind you to drink water could also double as a life-saving medical tool?  

Samsung, ever the pioneer, has finally unlocked its Galaxy Watch’s blood pressure feature—after years of being blocked by US regulators who, frankly, had no idea what they were doing. Now, users can calibrate their watches with a traditional blood pressure cuff (because nothing says “cutting-edge technology” like relying on 1970s medical equipment). The catch? You have to live in Brazil, or at least pretend to. Selecting Brazil as your region in the app somehow enables the feature, despite the watch being sold in the US. It’s like the watch is saying, “I’m not responsible for your hypertension if you don’t pick the right country.”  

Apple, ever the minimalist, has taken a different approach. Its new Hypertension Detection feature in WatchOS 26 is so groundbreaking, it doesn’t even give you your actual blood pressure. Instead, it sends you a vague notification that says, “You might have high blood pressure. Maybe check it? Or don’t, we’re not sure.” The feature requires you to wear the watch for 30 days before it even considers notifying you, because, as Apple explains, “we’re not here to help you, just to yell at you if we feel like it.”  

Meanwhile, YHE has taken the cake with its BP Doctor Fit, a watch that uses an inflatable cuff to measure your blood pressure. Because nothing says “medical-grade accuracy” like a device that looks like it was designed by a mad scientist. At just $129, it’s the perfect companion for your next family trip—just don’t forget to pack a portable air compressor.  

And let’s not forget Amazfit, which somehow makes its watches work for blood pressure monitoring only if you live outside the US. Because nothing says “global innovation” like requiring users to lie about their location to access basic health features.  

So, if you’re looking to stay on top of your cardiovascular health, consider investing in a watch that might or might not work, depending on where you live, what country you pretend to be from, and whether Apple feels like sending you a notification. Because nothing says “healthcare revolution” like a device that’s more confusing than a doctor’s appointment.
