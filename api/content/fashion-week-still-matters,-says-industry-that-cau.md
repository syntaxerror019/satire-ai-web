Fashion weeks are still the most important event on the global calendar, according to industry insiders who also happen to be responsible for 80% of global waste. While the world burns and drowns in climate disasters, fashion week remains the one place where everyone agrees on one thing: the importance of buying more clothes.  

“Do fashion weeks actually matter anymore?” asked one reader, who then immediately regretted asking because they now have to wear a $3,000 dress made of recycled plastic bags from the Great Pacific Garbage Patch. The answer, of course, is yes—because nothing says “urgent global crisis” like a runway show where models strut in outfits that literally scream “I was made in a landfill.”  

As for the industry’s environmental impact? “This is a tough problem,” said fashion expert Jess Cartner-Morley, who also happens to own a 27-piece wardrobe consisting of identical black blazers made from synthetic fibers that take 200 years to decompose. “But I believe it’s better to celebrate fashion than to disparage it. After all, who wants to be the person who says ‘I wore this dress once’ instead of ‘I bought this dress from a brand that caused a river to catch fire’?”  

When asked about the absurdity of promoting fast fashion while claiming to care about the planet, Cartner-Morley shrugged. “Sustainability is expensive, like actually caring about the planet. But we’re all just trying to make the world a better place—one polyester shirt at a time.”  

Meanwhile, the future of the French Tuck remains uncertain, but one thing is clear: fashion week will always be the place where the most powerful people in the world gather to decide what’s “in” while the rest of us try to figure out how to stop buying clothes that will outlive us.
