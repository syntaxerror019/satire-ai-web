In a stunning turn of events, Formula 1 has officially rebranded its entire lexicon to better reflect the visceral experience of watching cars go extremely fast and then violently crash into barriers. “Braking,” as defined by the sport’s latest “explanatory document,” now refers to “the act of screaming into the wall while simultaneously wondering why your life choices have led you here.” This redefinition comes as part of a broader initiative to make the sport more relatable to casual observers, who were previously confused by terms like “lap time” and “engine revs.”  

The future calendar, set to debut in 2026, will see races hosted in countries like Mongolia, where the only thing faster than a Formula 1 car is a goat on a skateboard, and the Arctic Circle, where drivers will be required to wear mittens made of melted-down trophies from previous seasons. “We’re excited to bring the sport to new audiences,” said a spokesperson, who was immediately fired for using the word “excited.”  

To commemorate its 75th anniversary, F1 has also announced a “retro-themed race” where drivers will compete in 1920s flapper dresses and must navigate a course designed by a man who once tried to build a time machine out of a toaster. Meanwhile, the “evolution of F1 design” has been rebranded as “the evolution of F1 design, but also the evolution of your ability to comprehend how anything stays on the road.”  

In a separate but equally baffling development, the sport has released a “key facts” section that includes information like “how many times a driver has accidentally eaten their own helmet” and “the exact number of times a mechanic has cried during a pit stop.” This data is now being sold as a premium subscription service, with the catch-all tagline: “Because who doesn’t want to know how many times Lewis Hamilton has tried to explain ‘hybrid power units’ to his grandmother?”
