In a stunning twist that will leave you questioning your life choices, the popular true crime podcast *Crime Junkie* has officially confirmed what we all suspected: people who enjoy true crime are not only real but also have a disturbingly specific obsession with cold cases, missing persons, and the occasional hero who “accidentally” solves a mystery while binge-watching Netflix. Hosts Ashley Flowers and Brit Prawat—best friends who have never met but communicate exclusively through a series of cryptic text messages—spend every Monday unraveling mysteries so convoluted they could only exist in a world where “cold case” is a euphemism for “we gave up and moved on.”  

The show’s latest episode, *“The Case of the Missing Sock,”* delves into the baffling disappearance of a single sock from a laundry basket in 1997, a mystery so mundane it’s already been solved by the laundry detergent company. Yet somehow, *Crime Junkie* turns it into a 45-minute saga involving a suspect with a PhD in “laundry theory” and a conspiracy involving a rogue dryer.  

Listeners are encouraged to “join a community of Crime Junkies” where the primary activity is debating whether a 20-year-old cold case is “still relevant” or “just sad.” The podcast’s sponsors, Audiochuck Media Company, have also launched a new line of merch: a T-shirt that reads “I Survived True Crime” and a stress ball shaped like a lock of hair from a suspect who “might” be innocent.  

As one listener, John Doe, said after episode 127: “I’ve never felt more connected to humanity. Also, I now know where my neighbor’s cat went in 2003. Thanks, Ashley.”  

If you’re tired of normal things like work, relationships, or sleep, *Crime Junkie* is here to help you instead obsess over the time a man was wrongly imprisoned for stealing a sandwich. Follow to join the ranks of the 97% of listeners who have considered joining a cult just to hear a new theory about a case that’s been closed for 30 years.
