Madison-area Rep. Francesca Hong, who somehow still believes she’s in a race for governor despite polls closing hours ago, has decided to double down on her delusional optimism by hosting a one-on-one interview with Isthmus at Atwood Music Hall. Because nothing says “political defeat” like a $10 ticket to a cocktail hour where you’ll be serenaded by a man who plays marimbas and probably believes in astrology.  

The event, which will feature a “cocktails and networking hour” starting at 5:30 p.m., is being marketed as a chance to “ask the state lawmaker, restaurant worker, chef, and former candidate for governor about the midterm elections, the state of the Democratic socialist movement, and her hopes for the future of state government.” But really, it’s just an excuse for Isthmus to charge people money while pretending to care about journalism.  

Hong, who has yet to concede her race (a move that has left her campaign staff questioning whether she’s been briefed on the concept of “losing”), will also field questions from the audience. This is a bold move, considering the only thing less likely to happen at this event than a Democratic socialist victory is someone actually asking her a question that isn’t about her “grassroots organizing movement.”  

The interview, which will begin at 6:30 p.m., will be followed by a Q&A session where attendees can ask Hong about her plans for the state’s dairy industry, how she’ll handle the “existential crisis” of not winning, and whether she’s considered running for mayor of a place that isn’t Madison.  

Tickets, priced at $10, are 100% tax-deductible and will “benefit Isthmus’ coverage of Madison news and culture.” Which, if you’ve ever read Isthmus, is basically a way to fund a staff of people who write about the same three restaurants over and over again.  

So come on down to Atwood Music Hall, where the only thing more surreal than a politician who won’t concede is the idea that anyone would pay $10 to hear a man play marimbas while pretending to care about politics.
