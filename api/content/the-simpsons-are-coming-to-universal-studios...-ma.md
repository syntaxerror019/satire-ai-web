At a recent D23 panel, The Simpsons showrunner Matt Selman casually dropped a bombshell: “We’re considering turning Universal Studios into a giant, haunted version of Springfield, but only if the ghosts of Marge’s past agree.” The crowd gasped—not because they believed it, but because they’d just realized they’re now legally required to attend a theme park that’s 90% nuclear waste and 10% Homer Simpson impersonators.  

Disney’s acquisition of Fox in 2017 was always a mystery, but now it’s clear: They bought Fox to finally figure out what to do with The Simpsons. Universal Studios Hollywood’s Springfield area has been limping along since 1998, surviving on the hope that someone, somewhere, still thinks “Lisa’s Brain” is a valid theme park attraction. Meanwhile, Disney’s Imagineers are frantically brainstorming ways to fit Springfield into Disneyland, possibly as a “land” where every ride is a 20-minute lecture on the dangers of donuts.  

Selman, ever the master of ambiguity, insisted, “I’m not sure if this is an official announcement or just me talking to a ghost in the back of the theater. But if it’s the latter, please don’t tell my therapist.” Universal Studios, for its part, is reportedly considering a Halloween Horror Nights attraction called “The Treehouse of Horror: Now With 100% More Horror and 50% Less Treehouse.” The experience will feature a haunted roller coaster where the track is made of Marge’s hair, a haunted corn maze where the scarecrows are all dressed as Burns, and a gift shop selling “D’oh! Cookies” that taste like regret.  

As for Disney’s future plans, insiders speculate that The Simpsons may eventually be moved to a new “land” at Disneyland called “Springfield: The Ride,” which will consist of a single ride that’s just a 45-minute line with a 30-second thrill. Until then, Universal Studios will continue to pretend that The Simpsons aren’t already a part of Halloween Horror Nights, because nothing says “thrilling experience” like pretending you’re not already dead.
