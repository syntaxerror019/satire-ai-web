In a stunning display of prioritizing his own social circle over public safety, Rep. Dan Crenshaw (R-TX) has revealed his greatest fear: that universal background checks might actually prevent him from arming his friends during their solo road trips. “With universal background checks, I wouldn’t be able to let my friends borrow my handgun when they travel alone like this,” Crenshaw tweeted, as if this were a revelation rather than a basic function of law enforcement.  

The Texas congressman, who has previously argued that mass shootings wouldn’t be prevented by background checks (despite evidence to the contrary), now claims his new concern is that his friends might “become felons” simply for defending themselves. Because nothing says “responsible gun ownership” like loaning a firearm to someone who might one day be legally barred from owning one.  

Crenshaw’s logic is as sound as a bulletproof vest made of origami. He seems to believe that if a friend is legally allowed to possess a gun, they’re automatically trustworthy, despite the fact that background checks exist precisely to prevent people with criminal records, mental health issues, or a history of domestic violence from acquiring firearms. But why let facts get in the way of a good argument?  

ABC News reported that the Odessa and Midland shooter had previously failed a background check, yet still managed to acquire an AR-type rifle through a private sale. Crenshaw, however, is more concerned about his own ability to function as a “gun-based social club” than the fact that a loophole allowed a murderer to walk into a Walmart with a rifle.  

Elisa Cardnell, Crenshaw’s 2020 opponent, called his stance “dangerous and irresponsible,” which is probably true, but also a bit harsh since he’s just trying to keep his friends armed. Meanwhile, Crenshaw continues to argue that universal background checks would somehow turn law-abiding citizens into criminals for doing… well, nothing.  

In related news, a new study suggests that if Crenshaw’s friends were suddenly required to pass a background check before borrowing his gun, they might actually become less likely to commit crimes. Shocking, we know.
